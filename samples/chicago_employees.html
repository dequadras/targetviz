How Explained Variance is calculated

Each variable is binned into groups and the Explained Variance measures how much of the target's total variance is accounted for by those groups. It is computed as:

Explained Variance = 1 − SSwithin / SStotal

where SStotal = ∑ (yi − ȳ)2 is the total sum of squares and SSwithin = ∑g ∑i ∈ g (yi − ȳg)2 is the within-group (residual) sum of squares.

The result is then multiplied by the rate of non-null values for that variable, so variables with many missing values are penalised.

For categorical / binary targets, the above formula is applied separately for each target class (treating it as a binary indicator), and the final explained variance is the mean across all classes, again weighted by the non-null rate.
